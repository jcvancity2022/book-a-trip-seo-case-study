Vancouver to Nanaimo ferry: routes, times, and cost

Two operators run this route: BC Ferries and Hullo. Here's what each one actually offers, side by side, so you can pick and book.

Ferry options between Vancouver and Nanaimo

Three routes cover this crossing today.

BC Ferries

BC Ferries carries both vehicles and foot passengers on two standing routes -- Horseshoe Bay (West Vancouver) to Departure Bay, and Tsawwassen to Duke Point -- plus a single seasonal direct sailing from Tsawwassen to Departure Bay (one departure daily, summer and holiday peaks only). Vehicle reservations became mandatory on the Horseshoe Bay route in October 2025, and are recommended on the others.

Hullo

Hullo is a foot-passenger-only, high-speed ferry running directly between downtown Vancouver and downtown Nanaimo -- no driving to a suburban terminal on either end.

How long does the ferry take?

  • BC Ferries, Horseshoe Bay–Departure Bay: about 1 hour 40 minutes
  • BC Ferries, Tsawwassen–Duke Point: about 2 hours
  • Hullo, downtown–downtown: about 70 minutes

Hullo's shorter crossing plus a downtown-to-downtown route usually means a faster door-to-door trip overall for anyone not bringing a vehicle -- not just a faster sailing.

How much does the ferry cost?

Fares checked 3 Sep 2026 -- confirm current pricing before booking, these change often.

BC Ferries: uses Saver Fares -- a standard vehicle and driver runs $49–99 CAD one-way depending on the sailing, or $110 at the prepaid/at-terminal rate. Foot-passenger Saver fares start around $15 CAD on select sailings. Fuel surcharges and terminal fees are added at booking.

Hullo: one-way adult fares range from about $20 CAD (Comfort-Connect) up to $60 CAD (Premium) before GST, across several tiers.

Ferry schedules and reservations

BC Ferries runs a minimum of 8 sailings a day year-round on the Horseshoe Bay–Departure Bay route, roughly every 2 hours from early morning to evening. Final departures from Horseshoe Bay typically range from 8:00pm to 10:40pm depending on the season -- there's no single fixed last-sailing time, so check the live schedule for your travel date rather than relying on a general estimate. Vehicle reservations are mandatory on the Horseshoe Bay route and recommended elsewhere; Hullo also recommends booking ahead.

Sailing times change with the season and can be disrupted by weather. For today's actual schedule, use BC Ferries' live schedule tool or Hullo's live schedule directly -- not this page.

Getting to and from the terminals

Once you're in Nanaimo, ride-share apps, taxis, and bike rentals all serve the ferry terminals. For a rental car, National and Budget both offer pickup directly from Departure Bay (and National also picks up from the Hullo terminal) -- Enterprise also operates locally, though not confirmed for terminal pickup. Worth calling ahead to confirm.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the ferry from Vancouver to Nanaimo?

About 1 hour 40 minutes on BC Ferries' Horseshoe Bay route, about 2 hours via Tsawwassen, or about 70 minutes on Hullo.

How much does the ferry cost?

BC Ferries uses Saver Fares: $49–99 CAD one-way for a standard vehicle and driver, or $110 at the prepaid/at-terminal rate; foot-passenger Saver fares start around $15. Hullo's tiers run about $20–60 CAD before GST. Both change often -- check the operator's site for current pricing.

What time is the last ferry?

Final departures from Horseshoe Bay typically range from 8:00pm to 10:40pm depending on the season -- there's no single fixed time, so check BC Ferries' live schedule for your exact travel date.

Can I bring my car on the ferry?

Yes, on BC Ferries. Hullo is foot-passenger only and does not carry vehicles.

Is there a faster option than BC Ferries?

Hullo's crossing is shorter and departs from downtown Vancouver rather than a suburban terminal, so it's often the faster door-to-door option if you don't need a vehicle.
